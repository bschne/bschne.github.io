A book on general principles of causality and causal inference
that doesn't get bogged down in the mathematics of this or that statistical method
seems way overdue.
Which is why it still annoys me how bad this one turned out.
Pearl spends considerable time talking about how nobody but him got the importance of causality,
and how they still haven't seen the light, and so on.
When he finally gets around to explaining the concepts,
he does such a bad job at it that I've told multiple people who thought this was out of their intellectual league
something to the effect of "it's not you, it's him".
